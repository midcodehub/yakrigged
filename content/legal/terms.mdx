## 2. DIY safety — read this even if you skip the rest

A lot of our content is hands-on: drilling kayak hulls, installing
flush-mount rod holders, wiring 12V electronics, building waterproof
battery boxes, mounting transducers. **All of this carries real risk**,
including but not limited to:

- Permanent damage to your kayak or accessories.
- Electrical short, fire, or battery damage (especially with LiFePO4 and
  lithium chemistries).
- Injury from power tools, sharp edges, or hot components.
- Loss of buoyancy or capsizing if a hull modification is done incorrectly.
- Drowning and other water-related hazards anytime you are on the water.

You — and only you — are responsible for:

1. Reading manufacturer instructions for every product before installing
   it.
2. Wearing a properly fitted **PFD (life jacket)** any time you are on the
   water. Our articles assume you do.
3. Working within your skill level. If you're not sure you can do a step
   safely, hire a marine technician.
4. Complying with local laws and waterway regulations (registration,

5. Inspecting your build before each trip.
