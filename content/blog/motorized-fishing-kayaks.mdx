## Read this first — the homework before the fun

A motor extends your range, beats wind and current, and — with GPS Spot-Lock — anchors you electronically so both hands stay on the rod. For big water and serious anglers, it changes everything. But four things trip up first-time buyers, so let's get them out of the way plainly:

- **Registration.** In most U.S. states, a motor turns your kayak into a registered vessel — electric or not. Check your state's boating agency *before* you launch. This is the single most overlooked step.
- **Weight.** Rigged motorized kayaks often run 120–160+ lbs. That's a trailer, or a cart and a helping hand — not a casual solo car-top.
- **Battery.** Almost never included. You'll add a 12V deep-cycle (SLA or lithium) and keep it charged.
- **Cost.** Premium integrated systems are a genuine investment — closer to a used boat than a kayak.

Get comfortable with those four, and the rest is upside.

## Integrated AutoPilot vs a trolling-motor kayak
