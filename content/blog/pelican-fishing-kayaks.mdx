## Why Pelican? The value math

Pelican's pitch is simple: get fishing for less. The Catch lineup is affordable, lightweight (so you can car-top and load it without recruiting a neighbor), stable enough for calm and moderate water, and stocked just about everywhere. For a first fishing kayak — or a knockaround second boat — it's genuinely hard to argue with the value.

So what do you trade away at this price? Be clear on it before you buy:

- **What you give up:** lighter-duty multi-layer hulls and lower weight capacities than premium brands' single-layer polyethylene, plus more basic fit and finish. You'll feel it in rough water and under heavy gear loads.
- **What you keep:** a stable, genuine fishing platform, light easy transport, and hundreds of dollars in your pocket.

For most recreational fishing on calm and moderate water, that's a smart trade. For pounding rough open water with a full rig, a premium brand earns its price.

## How the Catch lineup breaks down

Four families, sorted by how you want to fish:

- **Catch Classic** — the affordable paddle entry point; a straightforward, stable sit-on-top to learn on.
- **Catch Mode** — a wider paddle model built to stand and cast from, with a higher adjustable seat.
- **Catch PWR** — compact, light paddle kayaks with the Ergo360 seating system, easy to handle solo.
- **Catch HDII (HyDryve II)** — the pedal models, with a fin drive and (on the Kicker) hand-controlled rudder steering.

### Catch Classic 100 Angler (paddle) — best budget entry

Around $549, this is one of the cheapest genuine fishing kayaks worth owning. A stable 10-foot sit-on-top with Pelican's dual-position Ergocast seating — not a stripped-down toy, but a real fishing platform that gets a beginner on the water without a painful outlay. The kayak that turns "I've been meaning to try kayak fishing" into an actual Saturday.

### Catch Mode 110 (paddle) — best for standing

The Pelican to buy if you want to stand and sight-fish but would rather paddle than pedal. The Catch Mode 110 has a wide, flat deck and a higher ERGOBOOST seat designed for rising up to cast — stand-and-fish capability at a paddle-kayak price. Around $749.

### Catch PWR 100 (paddle) — best compact & light

A compact 10-foot paddle kayak with the Ergo360 seating system, easy to handle on the water and easy to throw on the roof afterward. A good fit for smaller anglers, tight creeks and ponds, or anyone who prizes an effortless solo car-top. Around $649.

### Catch Kicker 110 HDII (pedal) — best pedal with steering

A HyDryve II pedal kayak tuned for relaxed cruising, with hand-controlled rudder steering for smooth navigation through vegetation and skinny water. The pick if you like to pedal-and-explore as much as you fish. Around $1,799.
