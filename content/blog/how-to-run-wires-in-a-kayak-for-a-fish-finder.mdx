## The three places anglers actually run wires

Before we touch a drill, decide which routing path you're committing to.
There are exactly three:

### Option A — Through a sealed cable gland (recommended)

A purpose-built marine cable gland threaded through a single hole in the
rear hatch lid. One penetration, two cables (power + transducer), 100%
watertight once the gland nut is snugged down. This is the only method
I'd trust on a multi-day trip.

<ExpertQuote name="Marcus Reed" title="Lead Reviewer, YakRigged" source="12+ kayak installs, 2022–2026">
Always use an inline fuse between battery positive and the head unit — 3A for a 5-inch unit, 5A for a 7-inch. A short circuit on a 7Ah AGM melts insulation in 30 seconds without a fuse. The $4 inline ATC holder lives as close to the battery terminal as possible, not next to the head unit.
</ExpertQuote>

### Option B — Under the hatch rubber seal

Coil the cables flat and pinch them under the hatch lip rubber, exiting
where the seal compresses against the deck. **No drilling.** It works for
calm-water day trips but the seal fatigues over a season and you'll get
slow drips on rough water.

### Option C — Through a scupper plug

For sit-on-top hulls with a scupper near the seat, you can route the
transducer cable straight down through a scupper-mount transducer puck,
and the power cable up through a watertight scupper grommet. Skips the
hatch entirely. Only worth it if your hull is already designed for it
(Hobie Outback, Wilderness Tarpon).

## Step 1 — Plan the route on dry land

Put the kayak on sawhorses, seat installed, fully rigged. Sit in it with a
rod in your hand. Where does the head unit naturally land when you look
down? Where will the cables snag your line on a hookset?

Mark the head unit position and the cable path with painter's tape:

1. **Battery compartment** (almost always under the rear hatch)
2. **Up through the hatch lid** at the rear corner closest to your seat
3. **Along a deck rail / track**, *not* across open deck (snag risk)
4. **Up to the [RAM ball](https://amzn.to/4vw0yA3)** under your head unit

If your kayak has accessory tracks (most modern ones do), route the cables
parallel to the track and lash them every 6 inches with reusable cable
ties. Tracks are flat — cables hidden under them never snag.

## Step 2 — Drill the gland hole (this is the irreversible step)

Take your time here. PE plastic loves to grab a hole saw, and a 12 mm hole
in the wrong spot is permanent.

1. **Pilot first**, slow speed. PE plastic grabs faster than you'd think.
2. **Step bit at 500–700 RPM** for the main bore. Light pressure — let the
   bit do the work. Push too hard and you'll get a melted, glassy edge
   that won't seal.
3. **Deburr with a deburring tool or a sharp utility knife** rotated by
   hand. A clean, perpendicular hole = a clean seal.
4. **Test-fit the gland**. It should drop through without forcing, with
   maybe 0.5 mm clearance for the o-ring to compress.

> ⚠️ **Common mistake**: drilling through the deck instead of the hatch
> lid. The deck flexes when you load gear; the lid doesn't. Flex = micro
> cracks in your seal over time.

## Step 3 — Run the cables and seal the gland

1. Feed both cables (power + transducer pigtail) through the gland nut
   from the **inside of the hatch outward**. Length matters: leave 6
   inches of slack inside the hatch for service.
2. Drop the gland body into the hole, thread the nut on by hand, then
   tighten with two wrenches until the o-ring just starts to deform — no
   gorilla torque.
3. Connect the inline fuse to the battery positive **before** routing the
   power cable past anything that could chafe it.
4. Crimp the head unit's molex connector to the power leads. Heat-shrink
   the crimps with a real heat gun (not a lighter — uneven shrinking
   creates air gaps).

## Step 4 — Strain relief everywhere

This is where most DIY rigs fail after one season. Cables fatigue at every
point where they bend repeatedly. To prevent it:

- **Lash the cable to a deck rail or track** within 2 inches of the gland
- **Add a service loop** (3-inch loose curve) before the head unit
- **Never use bare zip ties**. They cut into the jacket. Use rubber-lined
  P-clamps or padded cable saddles instead.

## Step 5 — Pressure test before you fish

Plug the head unit in, power on, then dunk just the rear half of the
kayak in a kiddie pool / bath tub up to the hatch line. Leave it for 10
minutes. If water gets past the gland, you'll see it — pull the head unit
back off the mount and you can dry, re-grease the o-ring, and try again
before commit.

I do this on every install. It costs nothing and prevents the "ride home
from the launch with $400 of waterlogged electronics" event.

## Mistakes that cost me batteries (so they don't cost you any)

1. **Skipping the inline fuse.** A short on a 7Ah AGM dumps 100+ amps for
   long enough to melt insulation. A $4 fuse stops it cold.
2. **Mounting the head unit before final cable routing.** I once drilled
   the RAM ball into the deck, then realized the cable run interfered
   with the paddle stroke. Cost: one mount + one cosmetic plug.
3. **Wax-impregnated marine sealant on the o-ring.** It's for thread
   sealing, not o-ring lubrication. Use silicone grease on the o-ring
   only. Sealant on metal threads if you must.
4. **Coiling excess cable in the hatch and leaving it loose.** Eventually
   it ends up wrapped around the battery and pinches a lead. Lash any
   slack to a fixed point.
