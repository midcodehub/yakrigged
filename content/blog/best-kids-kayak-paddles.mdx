## Why weight matters more than length for kids

With adult paddles, length is the headline number. With kids, it's **weight** — and it's not close.

<ExpertQuote name="Marcus Reed" title="Senior Gear Editor, YakRigged" source="Editorial research, May 2026">
The fastest way to make a kid hate kayaking is to hand them a 38-ounce adult aluminum paddle. They'll fight it for ten minutes, get exhausted, and want to go home. Drop to a 16-ounce youth paddle and the same kid will paddle happily for an hour. The paddle is the difference between a fun day and a tow back to the launch.
</ExpertQuote>

A child has a fraction of an adult's arm strength, so every ounce counts double. That's why this guide leads with weight: a dedicated youth paddle is light enough that your kid actually does the paddling, builds confidence, and asks to go again.

## How to size a kids kayak paddle

Same two variables as adults — your child's **height** and the **kayak's width** — just scaled down. Use this as a starting point:

| Child | Age (rough) | Paddle length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small / first-timer (under 4'6") | 5–8 | 150–165 cm (60–65") |
| Bigger kid (4'6"–5'2") | 8–12 | 165–190 cm (65–75") |
| Teen (5'2"+) | 13+ | Move to adult sizing, 200–220 cm |

<StatBlock value="150–190 cm" label="the length range that covers most children's kayak paddles" source="Youth paddle sizing, 2026" />

Two rules of thumb:

- **When between sizes, go shorter** — the opposite of the adult advice. A child can't power a too-long, too-heavy paddle, and a slightly short one is far easier for them to handle.
- **Once a teen hits adult height**, switch to a normal adult paddle and use our [full kayak paddle sizing chart](/blog/what-size-kayak-paddle-do-i-need) — the kids' lengths will be too short.

## Safety first — the paddle is the easy part

A paddle is only one piece of getting a kid on the water safely. Far more important:

- **A properly fitted child's PFD**, worn at all times — this is non-negotiable and matters more than any paddle choice.
- **Close supervision** and calm, shallow water to learn in.
- **Sun protection** — hat, shirt, sunscreen.
- A **paddle leash** is a nice extra so a dropped paddle doesn't drift off.
